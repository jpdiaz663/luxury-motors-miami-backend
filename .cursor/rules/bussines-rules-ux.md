Business & UX Rules  ## 1. Vehicle Category vs. Physical Vehicle  The system must distinguish between:  * Rental Category. * Vehicle Model. * Physical Vehicle.  Example:  ```text Category: Midsize (ICAR)  Toyota Corolla Kia Forte Hyundai Elantra Honda Civic ```  These vehicles may represent the same rental category.  The customer should generally book the **category**, not a specific physical vehicle.  ---  ## 2. Category-Based Inventory  Availability must be calculated at the rental-category level.  Example:  ```text Midsize (ICAR)  Toyota Corolla       2 Kia Forte             1 Hyundai Elantra       2  Total available:     5 ```  The customer should see:  ```text Midsize 5 People 1 Large + 2 Small luggage Automatic  From $XX/day  [Book now] ```  The system internally maintains the individual physical vehicles.  ---  ## 3. Representative Vehicle  When multiple vehicle models belong to the same rental category, the UI may display one representative vehicle.  Example:  ```text Midsize Toyota Corolla or similar ```  The phrase **"or similar"** should be used when the customer is not guaranteed the exact displayed model.  This is particularly important when the fleet contains multiple models under the same rental category.  ---  ## 4. Specific Vehicle Guarantee  The system must distinguish between:  ### Category Reservation  ```text Midsize (ICAR) Toyota Corolla or similar ```  The customer is guaranteed the category characteristics, not necessarily the exact model.  ### Guaranteed Vehicle  If the business explicitly guarantees the exact model, the booking must store that guarantee.  Example:  ```text Toyota Camry — Guaranteed ```  The system must never imply that a specific vehicle is guaranteed when the business only guarantees the category.  ---  ## 5. Vehicle Upgrade  If the reserved category becomes unavailable, the system may assign a higher category according to business policy.  Example:  ```text Reserved: Midsize (ICAR)  Available: Full Size (FCAR) ```  The system may upgrade the customer without charging the additional category price when the business policy allows it.  The upgrade must be recorded in the booking.  The customer should be informed about the upgrade.  ---  ## 6. Vehicle Downgrade  The system must never silently downgrade a customer.  If the reserved category is unavailable and only a lower category is available:  * Do not automatically assign it. * Notify the customer. * Obtain explicit approval if required by the business policy. * Apply the appropriate price adjustment/refund.  ---  ## 7. Rental Category Attributes  Each category should define the characteristics guaranteed to the customer.  Examples:  * Number of passengers. * Large luggage capacity. * Small luggage capacity. * Transmission. * Fuel type. * Vehicle class. * Minimum/maximum age requirements. * Mileage policy. * Included insurance/coverage.  The displayed model is secondary to the guaranteed category attributes.  ---  ## 8. Category Codes  The system should support industry-style category codes.  Example:  ```text ICAR — Midsize FCAR — Full Size Car IFAR — Midsize SUV FFAR — Full Size SUV PFAR — Premium SUV LFAR — Luxury Standard SUV RFAR — Elite Standard SUV WFAR — Elite Luxury SUV GFAR — Elite Complete SUV LTAR — Luxury Convertible SVAR — Minivan ```  The category code should be an internal identifier and does not necessarily need to be exposed prominently to customers.  ---  # 9. Search Parameters  The rental search should support:  * Pickup location. * Different return location. * Pickup date. * Return date. * Pickup time. * Return time. * Promotional code. * Vehicle category.  The search should validate all parameters before querying availability.  Sky Miami's current search flow follows this model, including separate pickup/drop-off location handling, dates, times, promotional codes and category filtering.  ---  # 10. Different Drop-Off Location  The customer must be able to select:  ```text Pickup: Location A  Drop-off: Location B ```  The system must calculate any applicable one-way/drop-off fee.  The fee must be visible before booking confirmation.  ---  # 11. Minimum Rental Duration  The business must be able to configure minimum rental duration.  Example:  ```text Minimum rental: 48 hours ```  The system must prevent checkout when the requested rental period does not satisfy the minimum.  Sky Miami currently states a 48-hour minimum rental duration.  ---  # 12. Operating Hours  Pickup and return operations must respect location operating hours.  Example:  ```text Location: Miami  Operating hours: 06:00 → 22:00 ```  The system should prevent or flag pickup/return times outside operational hours.  The business may optionally support after-hours pickup/return with an additional fee.  Sky Miami currently publishes operating hours of 6:00 AM–10:00 PM.  ---  # 13. Driver Eligibility  The system must support configurable driver requirements.  Examples:  * Minimum age. * Young-driver surcharge. * Valid driver's license. * Identity document. * Credit card requirements. * Additional driver requirements.  Example:  ```text Age < 25 → Young driver fee ```  Sky Miami's terms currently require drivers to be over 21 and indicate additional coinsurance for drivers under 25.  ---  # 14. Authorized Drivers  Only drivers authorized by the rental agreement may operate the vehicle.  The system should support:  ```text Primary driver Additional driver(s) ```  Additional drivers may have additional fees or requirements.  ---  # 15. Mileage  Mileage policy must be explicitly defined per category or vehicle.  Possible values:  ```text Unlimited ```  or:  ```text 100 miles/day included + $X per additional mile ```  The mileage policy must be visible before confirmation.  Sky Miami states that most rentals have unlimited mileage while noting that restrictions may apply to certain vehicles or rental agreements.  ---  # 16. Fuel Policy  Fuel policy must be configurable.  Examples:  ```text Full → Full ```  or:  ```text Prepaid fuel ```  For Full → Full:  ```text Vehicle delivered: 100%  Vehicle returned: < 100%  → Fuel charge ```  The selected fuel policy must be shown during checkout.  Sky Miami's published conditions use a full-tank delivery and require the vehicle to be returned with a full tank.  ---  # 17. Late Return  The system must support:  * Grace period. * Partial-day charge. * Full-day charge.  Example:  ```text Return: 10:00 AM  Grace: 1 hour  10:00–11:00 No additional charge  11:01+ Partial-day charge  After configured threshold: Full-day charge ```  Sky Miami currently uses a one-hour tolerance, then charges half a rental day, and after five hours charges a full day.  These values must be configurable rather than hardcoded.  ---  # 18. Reservation Confirmation  A reservation request is not necessarily a confirmed reservation.  The system should distinguish:  ```text Reservation Requested ↓ Pending Confirmation ↓ Confirmed ```  The confirmation number should be generated when the reservation reaches the appropriate confirmed state.  Sky Miami explicitly states that a reservation request is not valid without confirmation and a reservation number.  ---  # 19. Price Lock  The system must support configurable price-lock behavior.  Example:  ```text Price shown: $500  Reservation not yet paid: Price may change  Full payment: $500 locked ```  The exact rule should be configurable according to the business's payment policy.  Sky Miami's terms state that prices may change without prior notice and that full payment freezes the reservation price.  ---  # 20. Payment Status vs Booking Status  These must remain independent.  ```text BOOKING Requested Confirmed Active Completed Cancelled No Show  PAYMENT Pending Authorized Paid Failed Refunded Partially Refunded ```  Do not use payment status as the booking status.  ---  # 21. Booking Expiration  Pending reservations should have an expiration mechanism.  Example:  ```text Reservation hold: 15 minutes  Payment not completed ↓ Hold expires ↓ Inventory released ```  The duration must be configurable.  ---  # 22. Filters  Fleet/search results should support useful filters such as:  * Category. * Vehicle type. * Passengers. * Luggage. * Transmission. * Price. * Features.  Filters must not display categories with zero availability unless the UX explicitly communicates that they are unavailable.  ---  # 23. Vehicle Card  A vehicle/category card should prioritize the information customers actually use to compare vehicles.  Recommended:  ```text [Vehicle image]  Toyota Corolla or similar  Midsize · ICAR  5 passengers 1 large luggage 2 small luggage Automatic  $XX/day  [Book now] ```  The card should not expose internal fleet identifiers unless operationally useful.  Sky Miami's fleet cards currently emphasize category, passengers, luggage capacity, transmission and rental action.  ---  # 24. "Or Similar" Rule  If the customer is reserving a category rather than a guaranteed model, the UI should make that clear.  Use:  ```text Toyota Corolla or similar ```  rather than:  ```text Toyota Corolla ```  when the exact model is not guaranteed.  This prevents a UX/business mismatch.  ---  # 25. Availability Message  Use category-based availability messaging.  Good:  ```text Midsize — Available ```  Not:  ```text Toyota Corolla — Available ```  unless the exact Toyota Corolla is actually guaranteed.  ---  # 26. Booking Summary  The final summary should show:  ```text Vehicle category Representative vehicle "or similar" status Pickup location Drop-off location Pickup date/time Return date/time Rental duration Mileage policy Fuel policy Insurance/coverage Extras Taxes Fees Total ```  ---  # 27. Rental Restrictions  Business restrictions must be configurable and validated before payment.  Examples:  * Geographic restrictions. * Cross-state/cross-border restrictions. * Age restrictions. * Vehicle-specific restrictions. * Mileage restrictions. * Authorized-driver restrictions.  Sky Miami, for example, states that its vehicles cannot leave Florida and that additional charges may apply for violations.  ---  # 28. Fleet Data Quality  Every vehicle/category shown to customers must have valid:  * Capacity. * Luggage capacity. * Transmission. * Category. * Image. * Pricing configuration. * Availability configuration.  The system should prevent publishing incomplete rental options.  This is important because the referenced fleet currently contains some entries with `0 People` and `0` luggage values, which demonstrates why validation should exist before publishing fleet data.  ---  # 29. Core Rental Model  The conceptual model should be:  ```text Rental Category         ↓ Vehicle Models         ↓ Physical Vehicles         ↓ Availability         ↓ Reservation         ↓ Physical Vehicle Assignment ```  The customer primarily interacts with:  ```text Rental Category ```  while the operations/admin system manages:  ```text Physical Vehicle ```  ---  # 30. Golden Rule  **Never promise the customer more specificity than the business actually guarantees.**  If the business guarantees:  ```text Midsize SUV 5 passengers 2 large luggage Automatic ```  the UI should communicate exactly that.  If the exact model is not guaranteed:  ```text Toyota RAV4 or similar ```  If the exact model is guaranteed:  ```text Toyota RAV4 — Guaranteed ```  The business logic, inventory system, pricing engine and UX must all use the same definition of what is actually being reserved.
